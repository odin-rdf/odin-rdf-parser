# Allocation guards and benchmarks for Turtle/TriG

## Parent Initiative

[[RDF-I-0003]]

## Objective

Extend the allocation-guard and benchmark machinery to the Turtle/TriG parsers and emitters: interning-aware allocation guards, interning-effectiveness measurement, and throughput baselines — closing the initiative with the same performance evidence RDF-I-0002 recorded in RDF-T-0011.

## Acceptance Criteria

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] End-to-end allocation guards over realistic corpora assert the full parse-loop profile: intern-table growth proportional to distinct IRIs only, statement-queue high-water-mark behavior, zero steady-state allocations in `parser_next` otherwise (complementing the per-component guards from [[RDF-T-0013]], [[RDF-T-0014]], [[RDF-T-0015]]).
- [ ] Interning effectiveness measured on a prefixed-name-heavy corpus: distinct-IRI count vs total IRI occurrences vs intern-table allocation count, asserted as a guard (allocations == distinct IRIs).
- [ ] Throughput benchmarks for Turtle parse, TriG parse, and both emitters over at least: a prefixed-name-heavy corpus, a structure-heavy corpus (property lists/collections), and a flat triples corpus; baseline numbers recorded in Status Updates.
- [ ] Turtle parse throughput compared against N-Triples parse of the equivalent flat corpus, quantifying the cost of prefix expansion + interning; result recorded.
- [ ] Guards and benchmarks runnable the same way as the existing RDF-T-0011 machinery (same harness/targets), CI-suitable.

## Implementation Notes

### Technical Approach

Reuse the RDF-T-0011 guard/benchmark infrastructure; add corpus generators for the three corpus shapes (deterministic, seed-free per repo convention). The N-Triples comparison uses the [[RDF-T-0018]] emitter to flatten a Turtle corpus into equivalent N-Triples input.

### Dependencies

[[RDF-T-0014]]–[[RDF-T-0018]] (parsers and emitters complete). Sequenced last with [[RDF-T-0020]]; independent of it.

### Risk Considerations

Guards asserting exact allocation counts are brittle against intern-table resizing policy — assert against the documented growth model, not magic numbers. If benchmarks reveal a regression-worthy hotspot (e.g., prefix-map lookup), file it as a backlog item rather than expanding this task.

## Status Updates

**2026-08-04 — Completed.** Corpus generators in `bench/corpus`, two new guards in `tests/guards`, six new benchmarks in `bench/main.odin`. Full suite green (5 guards, 10 harness suites, all unit tests).

- **Corpus design insight**: the guards work because the Turtle/TriG corpora are *fully cyclic* (period `TURTLE_CYCLE` = 1000) — statement `i` and `i+1000` are byte-identical, so after one cycle every distinct string exists and any later allocation is a regression. This sidesteps the exact-count brittleness the risk note warned about: no magic totals, just "zero after warm-up" plus one exact table-size check.
- **Guards**: `test_turtle_interning_and_steady_state` (steady-state zero allocations across 9k statements after a 1001-statement warm-up; intern table ends at exactly `TURTLE_DISTINCT_IRIS` = 1202 entries for 30k IRI occurrences) and `test_trig_graph_blocks_steady_state` (same across cycling labeled/default/unwrapped graph contexts). The structure-heavy corpus is benchmark-only: anonymous labels are per-occurrence interned strings by design, so it cannot be steady-state-zero.
- **Baselines** (M-series laptop, `odin run bench -o:speed`, 200k statements):
  - Turtle parse (prefixed-heavy): **7.18M triples/s** (117.9 MB/s, 600k triples)
  - Turtle parse (structure-heavy): **7.33M triples/s** (43.3 MB/s, 1.2M triples)
  - TriG parse (graph blocks): **6.54M triples/s** (117.6 MB/s)
  - N-Triples parse of the flat equivalent dataset: **10.57M triples/s** (859 MB/s) → **prefix expansion + interning costs ~32% in statement rate**, the ADR's anticipated allocating-path price
  - Turtle emit (prefix-aware): **10.62M triples/s**; TriG emit: **10.40M triples/s**
- One generator bug found while writing the TriG guard: `{` is an Odin `fmt` metacharacter, so the graph-block corpus emitted format garbage — caught immediately by the guard erroring at statement 1, fixed by writing braces outside the format string.
- No regression-worthy hotspot observed; nothing filed to backlog.
